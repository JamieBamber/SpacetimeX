QUASILOCALMEASURES SCALAR-OUTPUT REFERENCE
==========================================

Scope
-----
This note describes the formulas actually implemented in this adapted thorn,
principally in src/qlm_analyse.F90.  It is not merely a list of formulas with
similar names from the literature.  Cartesian components x,y,z are combined
where requested.

Conventions and notation
------------------------

S is the closed two-surface, parameterized by y^A=(theta,phi), with embedding
x^i(y^A).  The spatial metric and extrinsic curvature are gamma_ij and K_ij,
and K=gamma^ij K_ij.  The induced two-metric is

    q_AB = gamma_ij (partial_A x^i)(partial_B x^j),

and the physical area element is

    dA = sqrt(det q) dtheta dphi.

s^i is the outward-pointing unit spatial normal to S.  The null tetrad is
normalized so that

    t^mu = (l^mu+n^mu)/sqrt(2),   s^mu = (l^mu-n^mu)/sqrt(2).

The code uses G=c=1 and its extrinsic-curvature sign convention is
partial_t gamma_ij=-2 K_ij when lapse=1 and shift=0.  epsilon^ijk denotes the
flat Cartesian alternating symbol, delta_ij the flat Cartesian metric, and
x_0^i=(qlm_origin_x,qlm_origin_y,qlm_origin_z).  Repeated spatial indices are
summed.

Numerically, surface integrals are evaluated with the thorn's spherical
quadrature weights (described in the source as Driscoll-Healy weights).  The
continuous formulas below suppress that discretization detail.

Two broad warnings are essential:

1. Only the area, areal radius, and irreducible mass are intrinsic geometry
   without further choices.  Spins require a rotational vector field or
   coordinate rotations.  The finite-radius ADM and Weinberg outputs are
   coordinate-dependent diagnostics and become the corresponding asymptotic
   charges only under their usual asymptotically Cartesian/flat assumptions
   and in the large-surface limit.

2. In this implementation the ADM and Weinberg integrations use the physical
   normal s^i and physical dA even though comments in the code quote formulas
   conventionally written as n^i r^2 dOmega.  The conformal-ADM family instead
   uses an oriented coordinate surface element defined below.


GEOMETRY AND KERR-SHAPE ESTIMATES
---------------------------------

qlm_polar_circumference_0
qlm_polar_circumference_pi_2

    C_p(phi_0) = 2 integral_(north pole)^(south pole)
                       sqrt(q_theta_theta(theta,phi_0)) dtheta,

with phi_0=0 and phi_0=pi/2, respectively.  The factor two closes the meridian
through the opposite longitude.  These are proper lengths of two selected
coordinate meridians.  They diagnose shape distortion, but the choice of
meridian is coordinate dependent (and the two values need not agree for a
non-axisymmetric surface).

qlm_area

    A = integral_S dA.

This is the proper physical area of S.  For an apparent horizon it is the
horizon area.

qlm_irreducible_mass

    M_irr = sqrt(A/(16 pi)) = R/2.

For a black-hole horizon this is the irreducible mass: the mass remaining after
all classically extractable rotational energy has been removed.  It depends
only on horizon area.

qlm_radius

    R = sqrt(A/(4 pi)).

This is the areal (area) radius: the radius of a round sphere with the same
area.  It is not generally a coordinate radius.

qlm_spin_guess
qlm_mass_guess

These are Kerr-shape estimates constructed from A and the implemented
equatorial circumference C_e (qlm_equatorial_circumference):

    r_K = A/(2 C_e),
    a_guess^2 = A/(4 pi) - r_K^2,
    M_guess = (r_K^2 + a_guess^2)/(2 r_K),
    J_guess = a_guess M_guess.

The outputs are qlm_mass_guess=M_guess and qlm_spin_guess=J_guess.  Here a is
the Kerr specific angular momentum J/M, not dimensionless spin.  The formulas
invert the Kerr-horizon relations A=4 pi(r_+^2+a^2) and
C_e=2 pi(r_+^2+a^2)/r_+.  They are therefore only shape-based guesses and are
coordinate/tetrad independent only to the extent that the chosen equator is
geometrically meaningful.  If a_guess^2<0, the code returns a negative
qlm_spin_guess whose magnitude is sqrt(abs(a_guess^2))*M_guess; that negative
value flags an invalid (formally imaginary) Kerr spin estimate.


KILLING-VECTOR DIAGNOSTIC
-------------------------

qlm_killing_eigenvalue_re
qlm_killing_eigenvalue_im

These are the real and imaginary parts of a complex eigenvalue lambda of a
3-by-3 monodromy (holonomy) matrix, not a mass or spin and not a surface
integral.  For killing_vector_method="eigenvector", the code transports three
independent initial triples (xi^theta,xi^phi,chi) once around the selected
equator using the two-surface Killing-transport equations.  It diagonalizes
the resulting linear map and chooses

    lambda = argmin_(lambda_n) |lambda_n-1|.

The output is Re(lambda), Im(lambda).  An exactly single-valued transported
Killing field should have lambda=1+0i; the code rejects the field when
|lambda-1|>10^-4.  Thus these values are a numerical consistency diagnostic.
They are only assigned by the "eigenvector" method, so they should not be
interpreted when another killing_vector_method is selected.


ANGULAR MOMENTA (OUTPUT NAMES CALL THESE "SPINS")
-------------------------------------------------

Let phi^A be the normalized axial Killing vector (or approximate rotational
vector) found on S, and let its spatial push-forward be

    phi^i = (partial_A x^i) phi^A.

The normalization procedure aims to give its closed orbits affine length
2 pi.  The normal-bundle rotation one-form in the code's conventions obeys

    phi^i omega_i = -phi^i s^j K_ij.

qlm_spin

    J_phi = (1/(8 pi)) integral_S phi^i s^j K_ij dA
          = -(1/(8 pi)) integral_S phi^i omega_i dA.

This is the isolated-horizon/quasi-local angular momentum associated with the
chosen axial vector.  It is a signed angular momentum J (dimension mass^2),
not Kerr a=J/M and not dimensionless chi=J/M^2.  Its reliability depends on
the surface being sufficiently axisymmetric and on the selected Killing-vector
method and normalization.

qlm_npspin

With alpha_NP and beta_NP the Newman-Penrose spin coefficients and m^i the
complex tangent null vector, the code evaluates

    J_NP = -(1/(4 pi)) integral_S
               Re[(alpha_NP + conjugate(beta_NP)) phi^i gamma_ij m^j] dA.

This is intended to be the same isolated-horizon angular momentum as qlm_spin,
but evaluates the normal connection through Newman-Penrose quantities.  It is
therefore useful as a formulation/numerical cross-check and shares the same
dependence on phi^i and tetrad/sign conventions.

qlm_wsspin

The exact implemented formula is

    J_WS = (1/(4 pi)) integral_S zeta Im(Psi_2) dA,

where zeta=qlm_inv_z is an invariant axial coordinate constructed from the
Killing field by

    D_A zeta = (1/R^2) epsilon_BA phi^B,

with its additive constant fixed so that integral_S zeta dA=0.  This is a
Weyl-scalar/isolated-horizon spin estimate.  Caution: the source itself contains
an unresolved sign/factor comment immediately above this calculation, so this
entry reports the code exactly rather than asserting a convention-independent
identity with qlm_spin.

qlm_cvspin

The apparent intent (also stated in the run-time label) is an angular momentum
computed using the surface coordinate vector partial_phi in place of the found
Killing vector, i.e. something of the form

    J_partial_phi = (1/(8 pi)) integral_S
                         (partial_phi x^i) s^j K_ij dA.

However, the current source initializes qlm_cvspin to zero, never adds an
integrand to it, and finally divides it by 8 pi.  Therefore this output is
identically zero and does NOT currently calculate the displayed intended
formula.  If killing_vector_method="axial", qlm_spin itself uses
phi^A=(0,1) and supplies that coordinate-vector calculation.

qlm_coordspinx, qlm_coordspiny, qlm_coordspinz

Define coordinate rotation generators about the supplied surface origin:

    varphi_x^i = (0, -(z-z_0),  y-y_0),
    varphi_y^i = (z-z_0, 0, -(x-x_0)),
    varphi_z^i = (-(y-y_0), x-x_0, 0).

Then, for a=x,y,z,

    J_a^coord = (1/(8 pi)) integral_S varphi_a^i s^j K_ij dA.

These form a convenient coordinate spin/angular-momentum vector and do not
require axisymmetry or a Killing-vector solve.  They are coordinate and
origin dependent.  Their magnitude/direction is often useful in numerical
relativity, but they are not intrinsic charges on a general finite surface.

qlm_mass

    M_H = sqrt(R^4 + 4 J_phi^2)/(2 R)
        = sqrt(M_irr^2 + J_phi^2/(4 M_irr^2)).

This is the Christodoulou (horizon) mass constructed from area and qlm_spin.
For a Kerr isolated horizon it is the black-hole mass including rotational
energy.  It inherits any error or ambiguity in qlm_spin; if no Killing vector
is available, the code sets qlm_spin=0 and this reduces to M_irr.


FINITE-SURFACE "ADM" DIAGNOSTICS USING PHYSICAL dA
--------------------------------------------------

For these entries define h_mu_nu=g_mu_nu-eta_mu_nu.  In this routine the
four-metric used for h is explicitly reconstructed with lapse alpha=1 and
shift beta^i=0, irrespective of the interpolated lapse and shift.  Spatial
derivatives are Cartesian coordinate derivatives.

qlm_adm_energy

The exact implemented finite-surface expression is

    E_ADM[S] = (1/(16 pi)) integral_S s^c [
        delta^ab partial_b h_ac
        - gamma^ab partial_c h_ab
        - h_ab partial_c gamma^ab ] dA.

This is intended as an ADM-energy surface flux.  ADM energy is rigorously an
asymptotic charge; at finite radius this value is coordinate dependent and is
not the horizon/Christodoulou mass qlm_mass.

qlm_adm_momentum_x, qlm_adm_momentum_y, qlm_adm_momentum_z

    P_ADM^a[S] = (1/(8 pi)) integral_S
                     (K^a_b - K delta^a_b) s^b dA,

where K^a_b=gamma^ac K_cb.  These are finite-surface versions of the ADM linear
momentum flux.  They become ADM momentum only at spatial infinity under the
standard asymptotic conditions.

qlm_adm_angular_momentum_x, qlm_adm_angular_momentum_y,
qlm_adm_angular_momentum_z

    J_ADM^a[S] = (1/(8 pi)) integral_S epsilon^abc x_b
                     (K^c_l - K delta^c_l) s^l dA.

The contractions in the code raise the first index of K with gamma^ij.  Note
especially that this family uses the absolute grid coordinates x^i, NOT
x^i-x_0^i; changing the coordinate origin changes angular momentum by the
usual origin-times-linear-momentum term.  These are finite-radius,
coordinate-dependent approximations to asymptotic ADM angular momentum.


FINITE-RADIUS CONFORMAL COORDINATE-SURFACE FLUXES
-------------------------------------------------

These extra outputs deliberately use an oriented coordinate surface element,
not s_i dA.  Define

    dSigma_i = [ (partial_theta x) cross (partial_phi x) ]_i
               dtheta dphi,
    chi_raw = det(gamma)^(-1/3),
    tilde_gamma_ij = chi_raw gamma_ij,
    tilde_gamma^ij = gamma^ij/chi_raw,
    tilde_Gamma^i = tilde_gamma^jk tilde_Gamma^i_jk,
    chi = max(chi_raw,10^-4),
    tilde_A^i_j = gamma^ik K_kj - (1/3) delta^i_j K.

The floor is used only in explicit powers/divisions of chi; derivatives and
the conformal metric use chi_raw.  Orientation follows the parameterization
(theta,phi).

qlm_conformal_adm_energy

    E_cADM[S] = (1/(16 pi)) integral_Scoord chi^(-1/2) [
        tilde_Gamma^i + (2/chi) tilde_gamma^ij partial_j chi_raw
        ] dSigma_i.

This is a BSSN-like conformal finite-radius mass/energy flux.  It is explicitly
documented in interface.ccl as a diagnostic on a coordinate surface, and is
not an invariant quasi-local horizon mass.

qlm_conformal_adm_momentum_x, qlm_conformal_adm_momentum_y,
qlm_conformal_adm_momentum_z

The exact code can be written

    P_a^cADM[S] = (1/(8 pi)) integral_Scoord chi^(-3/2)
        [tilde_A^i_a - (2/3) delta^i_a K] dSigma_i

      = (1/(8 pi)) integral_Scoord chi^(-3/2)
        [gamma^ik K_ka - delta^i_a K] dSigma_i.

These are coordinate covector components (the free index is the second index
of the mixed curvature tensor).  They are finite-radius conformal diagnostics,
not invariant local linear momentum.

qlm_conformal_adm_angular_momentum_x,
qlm_conformal_adm_angular_momentum_y,
qlm_conformal_adm_angular_momentum_z

With X^i=x^i-x_0^i, the exact implemented expression is

    J_a^cADM[S] = (1/(8 pi)) integral_Scoord chi^(-3/2)
        epsilon^a_cl X^c tilde_A^i_l dSigma_i.

Unlike the earlier qlm_adm_angular_momentum family, this uses coordinates
relative to qlm_origin.  It is a conformal coordinate-surface diagnostic of
angular momentum and is coordinate/origin dependent at finite radius.


COVARIANT-INDEX QUASI-LOCAL LINEAR MOMENTUM
-------------------------------------------

qlm_quasilocal_momentum_covariant_x,
qlm_quasilocal_momentum_covariant_y,
qlm_quasilocal_momentum_covariant_z

    P_a^QL[S] = (1/(8 pi)) integral_S
                    (K_ab - gamma_ab K) s^b dA.

These are the Cartesian-coordinate covector components of the Brown-York/ADM
momentum surface density contracted with coordinate translations.  They use
the physical unit normal and physical area, unlike the conformal family.  The
word "covariant" refers to the lower free coordinate index a; it does not make
the three numbers coordinate invariant or define a unique linear momentum on
an arbitrary curved finite surface.  Raising this result after integration is
not generally the same operation as qlm_adm_momentum, whose index is raised
pointwise with gamma^ij.

These correspond to the quasi-local linear momentum defined in https://arxiv.org/pdf/0707.0876


WEINBERG PSEUDOTENSOR DIAGNOSTICS
---------------------------------

For these formulas h_mu_nu=g_mu_nu-eta_mu_nu, comma-alpha denotes a coordinate
derivative, and spatial repeated k is summed.  As noted above, this routine
forces g_00=-1, g_0i=0 and partial_0 gamma_ij=-2K_ij.  Consequently h_0mu=0 in
the actual calculation.

qlm_w_energy

    E_W[S] = -(1/(16 pi)) integral_S
                  (h_jj,i - h_ij,j) s^i dA.

This is Weinberg's energy pseudotensor surface expression evaluated on S.  It
is coordinate/background dependent at finite radius; asymptotically suitable
coordinates are needed for interpretation as total energy.

qlm_w_momentum_x, qlm_w_momentum_y, qlm_w_momentum_z

For component a, the literal expression accumulated by the code is

    P_W^a[S] = -(1/(16 pi)) integral_S { [
        -h_bb,0 + h_b0,b ] s^a
        + [ -h_a0,b + h_ba,0 ] s^b } dA.

Under the routine's enforced h_0mu=0 and h_ij,0=-2K_ij, this reduces to

    P_W^a[S] = (1/(8 pi)) integral_S
                    (K_ab - K delta_ab) s^b dA.

It is a Weinberg-pseudotensor momentum diagnostic, with lower/upper Cartesian
placement indistinguishable only relative to the flat background delta_ab.

qlm_w_angular_momentum_x, qlm_w_angular_momentum_y,
qlm_w_angular_momentum_z

First the code computes the antisymmetric tensor

    J_W^(ab)[S] = -(1/(16 pi)) integral_S s^c [
        -x^a h_0b,c + x^b h_0a,c
        +x^a h_bc,0 - x^b h_ac,0
        +h_0b delta_ca - h_0a delta_cb ] dA,

then outputs

    (J_W^x,J_W^y,J_W^z) = (J_W^(yz),J_W^(zx),J_W^(xy)).

With the enforced h_0mu=0 and h_ij,0=-2K_ij, only the two time-derivative
terms survive.  The x^i here are absolute grid coordinates, not relative to
qlm_origin.  This is Weinberg pseudotensor angular momentum and is coordinate,
background, and origin dependent on a finite surface.


QUICK INTERPRETIVE SUMMARY
--------------------------

Intrinsic surface geometry:
    qlm_area, qlm_radius, qlm_irreducible_mass.

Preferred horizon mass/spin when a trustworthy axial field exists:
    qlm_spin and the derived qlm_mass.

Cross-checks/alternatives for horizon spin:
    qlm_npspin, qlm_wsspin, and coordinate-dependent qlm_coordspin{x,y,z}.
    qlm_cvspin is currently nonfunctional (always zero).

Shape-only Kerr estimates:
    qlm_spin_guess, qlm_mass_guess.

Asymptotic-charge diagnostics evaluated on the chosen finite surface:
    qlm_adm_*, qlm_conformal_adm_*, and qlm_w_*.

Finite-surface coordinate components of a physical-normal momentum integral:
    qlm_quasilocal_momentum_covariant_*.
